## How Actual works (envelope budgeting; actualbudget.org/docs)
One handbook for the finance division (bookkeeper and advisor). Read every figure by these rules, and record entries (or plan them) so the rules stay true.
- Only money that exists is budgeted: To Budget = money in on-budget accounts not yet given to a category. Income counts in the month it is dated; unbudgeted money rolls into next month's To Budget. Budgeting more than exists makes To Budget negative and is taken from next month.
- Category balance = budgeted − spent + last month's balance carried in; a positive leftover always stays in the category. Overspending: read the category's `carryover` flag in getBudgetMonth. Off (the default): a negative balance at month end is reset to 0 and subtracted from NEXT month's To Budget (`lastMonthOverspent` in that month's getBudgetMonth). On (rollover overspending): the negative stays in the category into next month and To Budget is not touched. Say which when reporting an overspent category.
- Covering overspending or moving money between categories is a budget change (setBudgetAmount on both categories for that month), never a transaction. Months are not pre-filled: a new month needs its amounts set.
- Hold for next month moves money out of this month's To Budget into next month's; an unexpected negative To Budget in a new month can be a hold.
- Spending is net: refunds and returns are not income but inflows in the SAME category as the original purchase, with the merchant as payee (if two purchases could match, ask which); reimbursements owed to the user likewise go into the category the spending used. So `amount < 0` alone overstates spending: sum all amounts in expense categories (`transfer_id: null, "category.is_income": false`).
- On-budget accounts feed the budget and their entries take categories. Off-budget accounts only track a balance: their entries never take a category. Transfers between two on-budget accounts (paying the credit card, topping up GoPay or Flazz) have no category and are not spending. A transfer between an on-budget and an off-budget account is spending or income for the budget: its on-budget side needs a category. The user's usual one: `runQuery {table: "transactions", filter: {"payee.name": "<the off-budget account's name>", category: {$ne: null}}, groupBy: ["category.name"], select: ["category.name", {n: {$count: "$id"}}]}` (Loan Abi → Investments, Buffer / TabunganKu → Emergency Fund). Those moves are saving or lending, not consumption, and money coming back (Abi repaying) lowers that category's spent: getBudgetMonth's totalSpent mixes consumption and saving, so report them separately.
- The user's words: accounts go by short names (xpresi = BCA Xpresi, buffer = BCA Xpresi Buffer, tabunganku = BCA TabunganKu, cc or kartu kredit = BCA Credit Card); "ke X" is where the money goes, "dari X" where it comes from, never a payee. Abi paying ("Abi bayar", "cicilan Abi", "Abi transfer") is Abi repaying the loan: a transfer from Loan Abi to the named account, with Investments on the on-budget side; never a payee "Abi" and never an installment the user pays.
- Entries with no category (and not a transfer) are in no budget figure: count them separately and mention them, they make the month look better than it was.
- Credit cards and paylater stay on budget. A purchase takes its spending category when made; that is when the money leaves the budget, and the account's negative balance is the debt. Paying the bill in full is a transfer with no category: never count both the purchase and the payment. Interest and fees are spending in a category.
- Cleared = also on the bank statement; uncleared entries may still be pending. Reconciled = matched to a statement and locked: changing or deleting one needs the user's explicit OK first. Each side of a transfer has its own cleared and reconciled state and its own date; payee and amount are shared.
- A new account starts with its starting balance in the "Starting Balances" income category (leave that category out of income figures). Off-budget assets (stocks, reksa dana, gold, Loan Abi) show the balance of their last revaluation, not today's price: revaluing one is a single adjustment entry in that account for the difference, no category, with the new value from the user or a checked source, never invented. Net worth = every open account, debts negative; Loan Abi is money owed to the user.
- Rules run only when entries are imported (add_transaction, transfer, importTransactions), never on existing entries; a new or changed rule doesn't fix old entries by itself. Stages run pre → default → post.
- Split entries: the parent holds the total, the lines hold the categories. Queries return the lines by default; with `options: {splits: "all"}` add `is_parent: false` before summing or counting, or the figure doubles. mergeTransactions only merges two entries in the same account with the same amount (it keeps the imported one).
- A schedule matches a real entry within 2 days of its date (is-approximately means ±7.5%); auto-posting schedules record themselves on the date, the others wait for a real entry. Schedules are the known future: use them for cash-flow forecasts.
- Tags are #words in notes, case-sensitive (#kantor ≠ #Kantor), no spaces. Budget templates (#template lines in category notes) and Budget Automation are experimental, applied only from the app; the user uses neither.
